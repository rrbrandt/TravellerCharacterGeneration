INFORMATIONAL INTRODUCTION · ORIGINAL SYNOPSIS

01 / WHAT YOU’RE GETTING INTO

So what is Traveller?

Traveller is a science-fiction role-playing game. You play a person in that universe. The referee describes the situation, you decide what to do, and dice help settle the uncertain parts. The group finds out what happens together.

Your traveller already has a past: jobs, service, skills, maybe a pension, maybe a few bad decisions. That history gives you something to work with when the real adventure starts.

Book 0 is the introduction. It helps players and referees understand the game and get a campaign going. You don’t have to read every book before you can have a good evening playing.

02 / SAILING, WITH A JUMP DRIVE

Yes, think age of sail.

Think about sailing to a distant port in the 1600s or 1700s. You leave with supplies, a crew, and the best information you can get. By the time you arrive, that information might be old.

Classic Traveller has that same feel. Ships can jump between stars, but an interstellar message still needs a ship to carry it. You can use radio nearby; you can’t just call another star system and get an instant answer.

That gives the crew room to make decisions. The government may be powerful, but its instructions and reinforcements take time to arrive. Local officials, traders, and captains have to deal with what’s in front of them.

03 / WHAT DO WE ACTUALLY DO?

Get a job. Get curious. Get into trouble.

You might haul cargo, take passengers somewhere awkward, explore a new world, or accept a job from someone who leaves out an important detail. A trading run can turn into a rescue. A rescue can turn into a problem with the local government.

The ship gives you freedom, but it also gives you bills. Fuel, maintenance, and the next payment are good reasons to take a job. Your characters’ contacts and past careers give the referee plenty of ways to make that job interesting.

Here’s a place to start.

You’ve just landed. The cargo buyer isn’t at the dock. Someone offers to pay your fuel bill if you make one quick delivery first. The scout at the next table says the destination has gone quiet.

So, do you take the job? That’s enough to get the conversation going.
